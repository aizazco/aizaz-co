Seen isn't chosen: why reach alone won't sell a trip

Attention is the entry fee. Trust is the transaction.

Aizaz Sheikh · September 2026

A four-dollar bag of candy can sell out across a country because of one viral video. A six-thousand-dollar trip can't.

That sounds obvious. But look at how most travel marketing is still measured: reach, views, impressions, followers. We've spent a decade getting better at being seen. And in 2026, getting seen has never been easier. The feeds run on interests, not follower counts. A small operator in Patagonia can reach a hiker in Toronto without spending a dollar.

So here's the uncomfortable question. If reach is cheap and everyone can get it, why aren't more travel brands growing?

Because seen isn't chosen.

Attention is the entry fee

Think about how someone actually decides on a trip now. Call her Maya. A creator she's never heard of shows up in her feed, walking through Porto at golden hour. She saves it. Over the next ten days she saves three restaurants, searches TikTok for neighbourhoods "where locals actually live," reads the comments more closely than she watches the videos, and asks ChatGPT to compare two itineraries for someone who hates crowds.

By the time she reaches a travel brand's website, if she ever does, most of the trip is already decided.

Plenty of brands got Maya's attention along the way. Attention is the entry fee: without it you're not in the game. But attention isn't what she was buying. She was looking for something else.

Trust is the transaction

I saw this clearly while working with a large member-based travel organization. We went back through its own customer research: message testing across four very different traveller segments. The assumption in the room, reasonably, was that price would win.

Price mattered. Saving money and a price guarantee were strong reasons to believe. But the message that won, in every segment, was essentially this: no worries. Reassurance. People you can trust. Service you can count on when something goes wrong.

Those travellers weren't just buying a trip. They were buying confidence.

That's the part of travel marketing that reach can't do for you. A trip is expensive, it's rare, and it's emotional. People don't book it because a video made them laugh. They book when they feel sure it's right for them, worth the money, and that someone has their back.

The machine is reading your trust signals too

This gets more important with AI, not less.

When Maya asks an AI assistant for a small-group tour operator in Portugal, she doesn't get ten blue links. She gets an answer. Maybe three names. Your ad budget doesn't decide which three. The AI is reading the evidence about you: reviews, articles, creator content, forum threads, how clearly your own site explains what you are and who you're for.

If you sound like everyone else, there's nothing for it to pick. Generic brands disappear from the shortlist.

Three moves to get chosen

  1. Move your best asset earlier. Every travel brand has something genuinely special: the specialist who's been to Japan eleven times, the guide with two hundred treks behind them, the support line that answers at 3 a.m. For most brands, it shows up after the lead form, in the confirmation email or the welcome pack. That's the moment confidence could have been built, and it's already passed. Put your people in the discovery moments, where the decision is actually being made.
  2. Make trust visible from four sides. What you say about yourself. What experts say about you. What customers say about you. What the evidence shows. The brands that get recommended, by people and by machines, are the ones where all four line up.
  3. Measure confidence, not just attention. Views tell you that you were seen. Saves, shares, the questions in your comments and the conversations that follow tell you that someone is actually considering you. A save is a traveller saying, "I might really do this." Build your reporting around those signals.

Get seen. Then get chosen.

None of this means reach stops mattering. Interest still creates reach, and story still creates value. That's the foundation. But in a world where anyone can get seen, the brands that win will be the ones that give people the confidence to go.

Seen isn't chosen. The good news is that confidence can be built, on purpose.
